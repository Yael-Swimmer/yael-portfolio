Survivor Grandma

A magazine feature spotlighting grandmothers who refuse to slow down — illustrated with handcrafted paper cutouts.

Client Makor Rishon
Format Newspaper Feature
Discipline Editorial Illustration
Technique Paper Cutouts

She isn't sitting in a rocking chair. She's spinning a hula hoop — and rewriting what it means to grow old.

About the project

"Survivor Grandma" is a feature spread designed for Makor Rishon newspaper, exploring how today's grandmothers are redefining the chapter once labelled "old age." Instead of fading quietly into the background, they are travelling, exercising, building businesses, and showing up loud.

The illustration was built entirely from handcrafted paper cutouts — fabric prints for dresses, lined notebook paper for sleeves, magazine clippings for skin tones. The tactile collage technique was chosen deliberately: each layer is a small act of construction, mirroring the way these women have built their lives piece by piece.

"Aging isn't a quiet retreat — it's an active reinvention."

Visual approach

The composition centres a single character against a saturated yellow field — a colour chosen for its warmth and optimism. The hula hoop forms an unbroken oval of movement around her, while the floral skirt and striped blouse echo the layered textures of memory and identity.

Type direction was kept editorial and grounded: a strong Hebrew display face for the title, with body text running in clear columns alongside the artwork. The result is a feature that feels both contemporary and timeless — exactly like the women it portrays.

Outcome

The spread ran nationally in Makor Rishon, reaching hundreds of thousands of print readers — a feature article that put illustration at the centre of the editorial conversation.
